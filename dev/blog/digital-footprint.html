A Digital Footprint

Last updated: 2025-01-07

Preface

This piece of writing is what one might consider discursive. I have a general theme going, which is "how our real and digital lives interact, digitally and in reality". In saying that, I don't believe it is fair to say I am talking about one thing in specific, rather I use many of my views on the topics covered as a rationale for many of the decisions that I made to get to the point of writing something like this.

The forest for the trees

I have deleted almost all of my social media accounts, after much hesitation, because a good friend of mine told me it would make me feel better. He had done this much earlier than me, whether he had someone telling him to do it too, or if he was just a trend setter was not important for this story. He was preaching of the benefits of what I percieved to be such an extremely self destructive action. I could only see myself losing something by following in the act: friends that I haven't spoken to since high school or undergrad, photos that I never looked at but knew were always going to be there, or an entertainment sink that I could have ready to go whenever I was bored. He would tell me what a marvel it was to be able to focus properly again, to be able to read without fidgeting, to have more genuine connection with the people he would see in his daily life. I took this with a grain of salt. He was not a person that would use hyperbole when speaking of personal experiences, but I did not understand how such a step backwards could give me a more stable footing in life. It is interesting in hindsight, to see how we perceive our lives without social media. How would we ever stay in the know with the latest meme? After I had followed his advice however, I no longer see why I was so hesitant.

I don't assume common knowledge of concepts unless the topic is of the nature of discussion, this piece however is slightly different because of the less focused and very personal writing. One concept I would like to introduce is that of an "operant conditioning chamber", also known as a "Skinner box". Named after the American psychologist Burrhus F. Skinner, it is a lab apparatus that uses positive/negative reinforcement on animals to teach them to associate a specific action (like pressing a lever or pecking a disk) with a consequence (like food or a shock). It is primarily used to study behaviour in a lab, of which it is similar. The concept itself, being rather simple, has been extrapolated to outside of an academic environment to the commercial sphere, with companies utilising what is colloquially known as "the Algorithm" to direct user behaviour to drive further attention and engagement to content on platforms owned by these companies. It is a feedback loop that, if executed correctly on our hormonal brains, is essentially infinite money for any company willing to implement it.

Anecdotally, the people I ask tell me that most social media is used to "connect and keep up with friends". This can be done via direct messages, posts on a profile, or otherwise dehumanised, disembodied interactions such as "likes". The last one is what I can imagine most people interact with the most. As social media evolved the ways users interact with others on the platforms, the content that was given by companies was focused on short form videos. Comparing social media of fifteen years ago to today, and it is more homogenous in the experiences provided. A user will have their profile with followers/friends.
