Introduction

This guide is meant for those with none-little experience, but may be useful if you've ever had trouble working with Podman, DNS, HTTPS, and a few other goodies. If you know what you want to see, feel free to skip through to any section that tickles your fancy the most.

Building a personal network is an exercise in reclaiming digital sovereignty through a "Secure, Sustainable, and Simple" infrastructure setup. Relying on someone else's computer is no longer necessary as free and open source tools make it feasible to host your own services in your own software ecosystem without excessive cost or complexity. Moving beyond simple bash scripts, true resilience requires emulating the ideas from professional infrastructure to ensure seamless access across all your devices and for your family and/or friends. By adopting recent technology, a lone hobbyist administrator can maintain a robust system that rivals major providers while staying true to the KISS principle.

Layer 1: Physical

Network Topology

The first layer to our setup starts with the conceptual, how will the network look? It is easy to buy a spare computer and plug it in, but what about the external restrictions that you may not have control over? In Australia, where I live, our internet connections are notoriously bad no matter how much you want to pay. Not only are our speeds asymmetrical, which causes issues with out-of-LAN use, but most residential networks are CG-NAT by default for IPv4, with most ISPs charging an extra fee to break free. At least we have IPv6... Unfortunately, even this is spoilt by constantly rotating IPs, which can be a pain for public facing DNS usage, but that won't be a huge issue for me.

When I first starated in high school, my setup was in a residential network with 50/20 megabits down/up. However, as times change, they very much stay the same. I am now hosting from a residential network connection of 500/25 megabits down/up. The upload speed matters a fair bit when you are hosting, for the reason that you are no longer just consuming the data, but producing it too. A file download on your PC can only go as fast as the bandwidth that you are allocated from your ISP allows. This was fine, but as my list of users for my services has increased from family to now friends, the speeds are really the most limiting factor. This is however, the only external factor I cannot influence with money, time, or skill (legally).

Server Hardware

The server itself is an interesting topic. I always recommend to those getting into the hobby for the first time to buy secondhand. Specifically, office mini-PCs that will go to e-waste otherwise. Secondhand marketplaces will have many options, my first server was a used Dell Optiplex 9020 SFF. It was an amusing piece of hardware that survived for a long time.
